# Claude Cowork

**Author:** Nik Bear Brown  
**Publisher:** Bear Brown LLC

---

## One-Sentence Pitch

Claude Cowork is useful because it can act on your files; it is risky for the same reason, so the work begins with boundaries.

## The Argument

<!-- What does this book claim that isn't already obvious or settled?
     What changes in the reader's head between page one and the end?
     2–4 paragraphs. -->

Agentic knowledge work is not "AI does my admin." It is supervised delegation over files, folders, sources, and workflows. Claude Cowork can organize documents, extract data, assemble reports, and connect scattered materials — but only when the human defines the workspace, limits access, reviews the plan, verifies the result, and knows which tasks should not be delegated.

Most AI guides stop at better prompts and faster output. This book argues that the real discipline begins after the prompt: preparing a safe workspace, writing a task brief that names exclusions and review points, requiring a plan before Cowork touches a file, and documenting what changed and who checked it. Speed without that discipline simply accelerates error.

The reader who finishes this book will not just be faster. They will be able to decide what belongs in Cowork, what belongs in chat, what belongs in Claude Code, and what should not be delegated at all — and they will have a repeatable method for each category.

## The Gap

<!-- Why does this book need to exist? What does it do that no other
     book in the field already does? Name 2–3 books in the same space
     and say briefly how yours differs. -->

Claude chat guides teach conversations, not action over a workspace. Automation platform tutorials teach recipes, not judgment about what to delegate. Claude Code books teach developers. This book teaches non-coders — researchers, analysts, educators, project managers, and operations staff — who need agentic file and workflow support but have no intention of touching a terminal.

Unlike prompt guides that stop at better text output, this book treats Claude Cowork as supervised file and workflow delegation: a discipline with workspace preparation, access limits, plan review, verification, and workflow documentation at its core.

## The Reader

<!-- Who is this book FOR? Be specific — not "anyone interested in X."
     What do they already know? What are they trying to do?
     What will they be able to do after reading it? -->

The reader is a non-technical professional, researcher, educator, assistant, analyst, or project manager whose work lives in documents, notes, PDFs, screenshots, spreadsheets, and folders. They are comfortable using Claude chat but want help with the messy multi-step work that chat alone cannot finish. They know basic file and folder literacy; they do not know coding, terminal commands, or automation platforms.

After reading, they can prepare a bounded Cowork workspace, write an executable task brief, review a plan before Cowork acts, verify extraction and assembly outputs, organize files reversibly, and turn a successful task into a reusable workflow card.

## High-Level Outline

<!-- Three to five acts / parts / movements. Not chapters yet — those
     live in outline.md. This is the shape of the argument at altitude. -->

**Act One — Understand the Workspace** (Introduction, Chapters 1–3)  
The reader learns what Cowork can access, what it can do, what it cannot safely do, and why folder boundaries are intellectual boundaries. The first practical skill is not prompting; it is preparing a safe workspace.

**Act Two — Delegate a Workflow** (Chapters 4–8)  
The reader learns to write task briefs, require a plan, approve steps, inspect outputs for extraction and report assembly work, and run reversible file operations with a before/after manifest and human sign-off.

**Act Three — Build Repeatable Knowledge Work** (Chapters 9–12)  
The reader creates reusable Cowork workflows for research packets, operations support, and weekly reports, and completes a capstone that assembles and audits a full Cowork workflow end to end.
