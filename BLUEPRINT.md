# Part 1 - Book Concept and Thesis

## Book Concept Summary

This book teaches non-coding knowledge workers how to use Claude Cowork for file-heavy, document-heavy, and multi-step workflows while keeping human judgment in charge. It fills the gap between ordinary Claude chat and developer-focused Claude Code: the reader does not need a terminal, but they do need a discipline for granting access, approving plans, checking outputs, and documenting what changed.

## One-Sentence Logline

Claude Cowork is useful because it can act on your files; it is risky for the same reason, so the work begins with boundaries.

## Central Thesis

This book argues that agentic knowledge work is not "AI does my admin." It is supervised delegation over files, folders, sources, and workflows. Claude Cowork can organize documents, extract data, assemble reports, and connect scattered materials, but only when the human defines the workspace, limits access, reviews the plan, verifies the result, and knows which tasks should not be delegated.

## Thesis Test

- Act One teaches what Cowork is and when it is the right tool.
- Act Two teaches file access, task planning, output checking, and reusable workflows.
- Act Three applies Cowork to reports, research support, operations, education, and team handoff.

**Thesis test:** Pass.

# Part 2 - Learner Profile

## Primary Reader

A non-technical professional, researcher, educator, assistant, analyst, or project manager whose work lives in documents, notes, PDFs, screenshots, spreadsheets, and folders. They are comfortable using Claude chat but want help with the messy multi-step work that chat alone cannot finish.

## Prior Knowledge Assumed

- Basic file and folder literacy
- Basic Claude chat use
- Ability to review documents and spreadsheets
- Familiarity with the reader's own work domain

## Prior Knowledge Not Assumed

- Coding
- Terminal commands
- Claude Code
- Automation platforms
- Formal data-cleaning methods

## Prior Misconceptions

1. "Cowork is just Claude with files" - it is an agentic work surface with action planning and file access.
2. "If it can access the folder, it understands the project" - access is not interpretation.
3. "Automation means I do not need to inspect the result" - automation makes inspection more important.
4. "All repetitive work is safe to delegate" - sensitive, regulated, irreversible, and ambiguous work needs extra constraints or no delegation.

## Motivation Type

**Primary:** Professional productivity.  
**Secondary:** Research and education support.  
**Not primary:** Software development.

# Part 3 - Book Type and Deployment Specification

## Book Type

**Primary type:** Practitioner handbook.  
**Secondary type:** Workplace AI training text.  
**Not:** A coding book, a general AI theory text, or a compliance manual.

## Primary Adoption Context

Self-directed professionals and teams learning how to delegate file-heavy work responsibly.

## Secondary Adoption Context

University staff/faculty workshops, research assistant training, operations teams, nonprofits, and small organizations with document-heavy workflows.

## What This Book Is Not Designed For

- Regulated workloads requiring formal compliance approval
- Replacing organizational document-control policy
- Fully autonomous workflow execution without review
- Developers who need Claude Code

# Part 4 - Field Positioning

## Comparable Texts and Gaps

**Claude chat guides** teach conversations, not action over a workspace.  
**Automation platform tutorials** teach recipes, not judgment about what to delegate.  
**Claude Code books** teach developers. This book teaches non-coders who need agentic workflow support.

## Positioning Statement

Unlike prompt guides that stop at better text output, this book teaches Claude Cowork as supervised file and workflow delegation for people whose work is scattered across documents, folders, spreadsheets, and notes.

# Part 5 - Three-Act Learning Arc

## Act One - Understand the Workspace

The reader learns what Cowork can access, what it can do, what it cannot safely do, and why folder boundaries are intellectual boundaries.

## Act Two - Delegate a Workflow

The reader learns to prepare source materials, write task briefs, require a plan, approve steps, and inspect the output.

## Act Three - Build Repeatable Knowledge Work

The reader creates reusable Cowork workflows for reports, data extraction, folder cleanup, research packets, and team handoff.

## Arc Statement

This book takes the reader from scattered files and vague delegation to bounded, repeatable Cowork workflows by first defining the workspace, then supervising the task, then documenting the workflow for reuse.

# Part 7 - Chapter-by-Chapter TOC

## Introduction - The Work Hidden in Your Folders

**Capability built:** Identify Cowork-shaped work.

The introduction names the work Cowork is built for: document packets, scattered notes, messy folders, recurring reports, screenshots, spreadsheets, and multi-step preparation tasks.

## Chapter 1 - What Cowork Is For

**Capability built:** Decide whether a task belongs in Cowork, Claude chat, Claude Code, or nowhere.

The chapter introduces the task triage: conversation, codebase action, file/workflow action, and human-only judgment.

## Chapter 2 - Preparing a Safe Workspace

**Capability built:** Create a bounded folder and access plan.

The reader learns to copy working files, remove sensitive materials, define allowed folders, and document what Cowork is allowed to touch.

## Chapter 3 - Connectors, Local Files, and Permissions

**Capability built:** Understand and constrain Cowork's access.

The chapter explains local files, connectors, cloud sources, browser assistance, and the principle of least privilege.

## Chapter 4 - The Cowork Task Brief

**Capability built:** Write a brief that turns messy intent into an executable plan.

Core elements: objective, source materials, exclusions, output format, decision rules, review points, and stop conditions.

## Chapter 5 - Plans, Approvals, and Redirection

**Capability built:** Review Cowork's plan before action.

The reader learns to identify missing steps, risky steps, wrong assumptions, and places where human judgment must be inserted.

## Chapter 6 - Extracting Data from Documents

**Capability built:** Use Cowork for extraction while checking the rows.

Examples include receipts, invoices, screenshots, PDFs, forms, and handwritten-looking source problems. Verification includes row counts, spot checks, and exception logs.

## Chapter 7 - Assembling Reports and Decks

**Capability built:** Turn source materials into a structured report or presentation draft.

The reader learns to provide templates, voice constraints, citation/source requirements, and review checkpoints.

## Chapter 8 - Organizing and Renaming Files

**Capability built:** Run reversible file organization work safely.

The chapter teaches dry runs, naming conventions, before/after manifests, backups, and human approval before bulk changes.

## Chapter 9 - Research Packets and Meeting Notes

**Capability built:** Convert scattered notes into usable research or meeting artifacts.

Examples include literature lead packets, meeting summaries, decision logs, action items, and briefing documents.

## Chapter 10 - What Not to Delegate

**Capability built:** Refuse or redesign unsafe Cowork tasks.

The chapter covers regulated data, legal/medical/financial risk, private student data, personnel decisions, irreversible file operations, and ambiguous source material.

## Chapter 11 - Building a Reusable Cowork Workflow

**Capability built:** Turn a successful task into a repeatable standard operating procedure.

The reader creates a workflow card with inputs, folder structure, task brief, verification checklist, and handoff note.

## Chapter 12 - Capstone: The Weekly Operations Packet

**Capability built:** Execute and verify a complete Cowork workflow.

The capstone has the reader assemble a report from notes, emails, data snippets, and source files, then produce an audit note documenting what Cowork did and what the human verified.

# Part 10 - Hard Topics and Aging Risk

## Hard Chapters

- **Chapter 3:** Product access and connector details may change.
- **Chapter 6:** Extraction workflows must not imply unverified data accuracy.
- **Chapter 10:** Safety guidance must be conservative but usable.

## Aging Risk

High-aging-risk content includes interface details, connector availability, product status, and supported integrations. Stable content includes workspace preparation, least privilege, plan review, reversible operations, and verification.

# Part 11 - Adoption Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cowork features change | High | Medium | Keep screenshots/interface notes out of core logic |
| Reader overdelegates sensitive work | Medium | High | Put boundaries early and repeat them |
| Reader expects magic automation | High | Medium | Require task briefs and verification |
| Non-coders feel intimidated | Medium | Medium | Avoid terminal/coding assumptions |
